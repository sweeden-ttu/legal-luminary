profile_summary: |
  Paul L. LePak is the judge of the 264th Judicial District Court in Bell County ([Bell County](https://www.bellcountytx.com/county_government/district_courts/264th_district_court/index.php)). In May 2018 he defeated Jeff Parker in the Republican primary runoff for the seat, and on June 25, 2018, Gov. Greg Abbott appointed him to serve the rest of the term of Judge Martha Trudo, who had retired earlier that year after 23 years on the bench; he faced no opponent in November 2018 ([KWTX](https://www.kwtx.com/content/news/Governor-appoints-runoff-winner-to-fill-vacant-local-court-seat-486472521.html)). The appointment was for a term set to expire Dec. 31, 2018, or until his successor was elected and qualified ([Office of the Governor](https://gov.texas.gov/news/post/governor-abbott-appoints-lepak-judge-of-the-264th-judicial-district-court)). In the November 2022 general election he was unopposed and declared elected ([Texas Secretary of State](https://newtools.cira.state.tx.us/upload/page/10782/docs/2022%20-%20NOVEMBER%208%20-%20GENERAL/BALLOT%20INFO/2022%20-%20NOVEMBER%208%20-%20STATEWIDE%20DISTRICT%20UNOPPOSED%20DECLARED%20ELECTED%20CERTIFICATION%20-%20REVISED.pdf)). District judges in Texas serve four-year terms ([Texas Constitution, art. V, sec. 7](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/cn/htm/cn.5.htm)).

  At the time of his appointment, LePak was a Temple attorney in private practice. He holds a B.A. in political science from Marquette University and a J.D. from Villanova University School of Law. He was a member of the State Bar of Texas, the Bell County Bar Association and the Texas Criminal Defense Lawyers Association, a fellow of the Texas Bar Foundation, and a former president and treasurer of the Bell County Criminal Defense Lawyers Association ([Office of the Governor](https://gov.texas.gov/news/post/governor-abbott-appoints-lepak-judge-of-the-264th-judicial-district-court)).

  **2026 election:** He was unopposed in the March 2026 Republican primary, and no Democrat filed ([KDH](https://kdhnews.com/news/region/locals-vying-for-county-seats-in-primary-elections/article_6337d52a-83e9-5b75-8efe-8f6d35b2a54b.html)). He is the unopposed candidate for the 264th Judicial District on the Nov. 3, 2026 ballot ([Nov. 3 ballot guide](/nov-3-2026-ballot-guide/)).
