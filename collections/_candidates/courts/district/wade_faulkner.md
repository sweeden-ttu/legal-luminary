profile_summary: |
  Wade Faulkner is the judge of the 478th Judicial District Court in Bell County ([Bell County](https://www.bellcountytx.com/county_government/district_courts/478th_judicial_district_court/index.php)). The 478th Judicial District, composed of Bell County, was created by the Legislature effective Jan. 1, 2022 ([H.B. 3774, 87th Legislature](https://legiscan.com/TX/text/HB3774/2021)). On Aug. 9, 2022, the Bell County Republican Party's district executive committee chose Faulkner from three candidates as its nominee for the 478th District Judge seat on the November 2022 ballot ([KCEN](https://www.kcentv.com/article/news/politics/wade-faulkner-wins-2022-nomination-478th-district-judge/500-f7021997-b9cb-47f6-a987-123f8ea0aade)). On Aug. 18, 2022, Gov. Greg Abbott appointed him to the court for a term set to expire Dec. 31, 2022, or until his successor was elected and qualified ([Office of the Governor](https://gov.texas.gov/news/post/governor-abbott-appoints-faulkner-to-478th-judicial-district-court)). In the November 2022 general election he was unopposed and declared elected ([Texas Secretary of State](https://newtools.cira.state.tx.us/upload/page/10782/docs/2022%20-%20NOVEMBER%208%20-%20GENERAL/BALLOT%20INFO/2022%20-%20NOVEMBER%208%20-%20STATEWIDE%20DISTRICT%20UNOPPOSED%20DECLARED%20ELECTED%20CERTIFICATION%20-%20REVISED.pdf)). District judges in Texas serve four-year terms ([Texas Constitution, art. V, sec. 7](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/cn/htm/cn.5.htm)).

  At the time of his appointment, Faulkner, of Salado, was an attorney and partner at the Law Office of Wade Faulkner, PLLC, and a former U.S. Army Judge Advocate with more than 20 years of experience in criminal law; he served in the Army for over 23 years before retiring in 2016 ([Office of the Governor](https://gov.texas.gov/news/post/governor-abbott-appoints-faulkner-to-478th-judicial-district-court)). As a military judge he presided over the death penalty trial of Fort Hood shooter Nidal Hasan ([KDH](https://kdhnews.com/centerforpolitics/three-republicans-vying-for-426th-district-court-judgeship/article_d9f35960-4f74-11ea-9617-2790fe7a8695.html)). He holds a B.A. in history from Texas Tech University, a J.D. from Baylor Law School, and an LL.M. in military law from the Judge Advocate General's Legal Center and School ([Office of the Governor](https://gov.texas.gov/news/post/governor-abbott-appoints-faulkner-to-478th-judicial-district-court)). In 2020 he ran in the Republican primary for the 426th District Court, finishing third with 23% of the vote ([KDH](https://kdhnews.com/centerforpolitics/duskie-parker-to-vie-for-426th-judge-runoff/article_89f90810-3e0d-5b25-bd1a-5210e68d751d.html)). According to his campaign website, he also serves as judge of the Bell County Veterans Treatment Court ([campaign website](https://wadefaulkner.com)).

  **2026 election:** He was unopposed in the March 2026 Republican primary, and no Democrat filed ([KDH](https://kdhnews.com/news/region/locals-vying-for-county-seats-in-primary-elections/article_6337d52a-83e9-5b75-8efe-8f6d35b2a54b.html)). He was the unopposed candidate for the 478th Judicial District on the Nov. 3, 2026 ballot ([Nov. 3 ballot guide](/nov-3-2026-ballot-guide/)).

  **Nov. 3, 2026 result:** Wade Faulkner was the only candidate for District Judge, 478th Judicial District on the Nov. 3, 2026 ballot and received 69,125 votes in unofficial returns ([Texas Secretary of State](https://electionresults.sos.state.tx.us/results.html)).
