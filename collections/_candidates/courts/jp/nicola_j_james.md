profile_summary: |
  Nicola J. James is the incumbent Justice of the Peace for Bell County Precinct 4, Place 2; Bell County lists her court at the Bell County Killeen Annex, 301 Priest Drive, Killeen ([Bell County](https://www.bellcountytx.com/county_government/justice_of_the_peace/precinct_4_place_2/index.php)). A Democrat, she was elected in 2022 to replace longtime Judge Bill Cooke, now the Bell County sheriff; the precinct covers nearly all of Harker Heights and Killeen. Before taking the bench she was a court administrator in Copperas Cove ([Killeen Daily Herald](https://kdhnews.com/centerforpolitics/judge-nicola-james-to-run-for-reelection/article_ee7582ef-f935-42c4-ac49-f35fc7ab5748.html)).

  **2026 election:** James sought re-election in 2026, announcing her campaign in October 2025 ([KDH](https://kdhnews.com/centerforpolitics/judge-nicola-james-to-run-for-reelection/article_ee7582ef-f935-42c4-ac49-f35fc7ab5748.html)). In the March 3, 2026 Democratic primary she finished third with 3,679 votes (27.86%), behind LaTasha Quarles (5,110; 38.70%) and Jessica Gonzalez (4,415; 33.44%), who advanced to the May 26 runoff ([KDH](https://kdhnews.com/centerforpolitics/jp-for-killeen-quarles-gonzalez-head-to-runoff-election-as-incumbent-finishes-3rd/article_baed7726-ac7b-42e9-bfc8-1a9515ac424c.html)). Gonzalez won the runoff and faces Republican Beatrice Cox on the Nov. 3, 2026 ballot ([KDH](https://kdhnews.com/centerforpolitics/gonzalez-a-killeen-councilwoman-wins-democratic-jp-primary-runoff/article_86fc56ce-ab7a-4d23-8bdc-d4b7b1a1bf86.html); [Nov. 3 ballot guide](/nov-3-2026-ballot-guide/)).
