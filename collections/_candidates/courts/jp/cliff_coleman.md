profile_summary: |
  Cliff Coleman served as the Justice of the Peace for Bell County Precinct 2. He took the oath of office on Jan. 1, 2019, following a temporary appointment, succeeding Don Engleking, who had held the post for 48 years; he won a Republican primary and had no general-election challenger ([Killeen Daily Herald](https://kdhnews.com/news/region/justice-of-the-peace-coleman-won-t-seek-re-election-in-2026/article_21f1fef2-5caa-54d8-8812-d897731347d3.html)). The Herald has described the precinct as covering areas in and around Salado, Holland, Bartlett, the bottom portion of Belton and the south part of Bell County ([KDH](https://kdhnews.com/news/region/patterson-leads-justice-of-the-peace-precinct-1-race-sapp-tice-lead-four-way-jp/article_d48a29aa-236d-5573-b918-1c016d6d81d1.html)).

  Coleman announced before the filing period that he would not seek re-election in 2026; among the accomplishments he cited were presiding over Bell County Truancy Court for four and a half years and completing the Bell County Salado Annex ([KDH](https://kdhnews.com/news/region/justice-of-the-peace-coleman-won-t-seek-re-election-in-2026/article_21f1fef2-5caa-54d8-8812-d897731347d3.html)). On June 23, 2026, he announced he would resign effective July 20, 2026 to take a position with the Bell County Sheriff's Office at the request of Sheriff Bill Cooke; he said he expected county officials to appoint Republican runoff winner Michael Tice to the seat ([KDH](https://kdhnews.com/news/region/judge-cliff-coleman-announces-resignation-move-to-sheriffs-office/article_26c48db0-d050-5eaf-924f-079fb4b118eb.html)). By August 2026 he was the Sheriff's Office public information officer ([KDH](https://kdhnews.com/news/region/two-killed-in-apache-helicopter-crash-near-salado-causing-fire-that-forces-evacuations/article_4a10c5ee-9704-547e-992a-21e178dcb999.html)). Bell County now lists Michael Tice as the Precinct 2 judge ([Bell County](https://www.bellcountytx.com/county_government/justice_of_the_peace/precinct_2/index.php)).
