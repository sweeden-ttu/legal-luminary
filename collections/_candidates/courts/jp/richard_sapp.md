profile_summary: |
  Richard Sapp was a Republican candidate for Justice of the Peace, Precinct 2 in Bell County in 2026. He lost the May 26, 2026 Republican primary runoff to Michael Tice, 1,111 votes to 1,286 ([Killeen Daily Herald](https://kdhnews.com/news/region/low-turnout-high-stakes-bell-county-republicans-lean-conservative-in-runoff-election/article_99d45d87-32ee-5d96-84d9-4a50afee7f74.html)). 
  In the March 3, 2026 four-way Republican primary, Sapp finished first with 1,308 votes to Tice's 1,219 (unofficial); no candidate won a majority, so the two went to a runoff ([KDH](https://kdhnews.com/news/region/sapp-tice-to-battle-in-precinct-2-justice-of-the-peace-runoff/article_2d0c225d-407a-53ed-a85b-57304ed2098b.html)). Sapp entered the race after incumbent Cliff Coleman announced he would not seek re-election; he had also run for the seat in 2022, losing the Republican primary to Coleman by 385 votes ([KDH](https://kdhnews.com/news/region/sapp-announces-run-for-justice-of-the-peace-after-coleman-announces-retirement/article_74677e2f-6347-5043-a76a-96e6d1d42959.html)).

  Sapp, a Salado resident, spent 32 years as a detective with the Temple Police Department and taught criminal justice at Central Texas College for 20 years. He lists memberships in the Justice of the Peace and Constables Association of Texas, the Texas Municipal Police Association and the American Bar Association ([KDH](https://kdhnews.com/news/region/four-republicans-vying-for-bell-county-justice-of-the-peace-precinct-no-2-primary-election/article_c32ac422-0725-5baa-9be7-78fa9240d061.html)). The Herald reported that he holds a master's degree in criminal justice from Tarleton State University and a bachelor's degree from Regents College, and attended Texas A&M School of Law ([KDH](https://kdhnews.com/news/region/sapp-announces-run-for-justice-of-the-peace-after-coleman-announces-retirement/article_74677e2f-6347-5043-a76a-96e6d1d42959.html)).
