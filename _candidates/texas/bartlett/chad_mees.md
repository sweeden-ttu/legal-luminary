# Executive Summary

Chad Mees serves as the Mayor of Bartlett, a Type A General Law city in Texas. He was re-elected to a two-year term running from 2024 to 2026, having previously taken an oath of office on May 16, 2022. As mayor, Mees has been publicly involved in several key local issues. He has actively explained the city's move towards a more formal building code and permitting process, contrasting it with the previous, less regulated system. Mees has also participated in community meetings concerning Bartlett ISD's proposed $16.8 million bond, where he addressed resident questions about project costs influenced by the new city codes. In matters of public safety, he provided official updates during an investigation into a series of arsons, confirming that investigators had identified a pattern linking several incidents to a single suspect. He also attended a town hall on the school bond where the superintendent was absent, leaving many resident questions unanswered, though his role was as a city representative rather than a school district proponent.

# Key Priorities And Stances

Chad Mees's priorities as Mayor of Bartlett, Texas, are centered on city governance, local education infrastructure, and public safety. A key priority has been modernizing city standards, specifically by implementing a more formal and stringent building code and permitting process. He publicly defended this change, contrasting it with the city's previous, more lenient system where a permit cost '$10 bucks, no inspection, no rules to follow basically.' This indicates a focus on structured development and safety. Additionally, Mees has been actively involved in discussions surrounding the Bartlett Independent School District's (ISD) financial matters, participating in community meetings for a proposed $16.8 million bond to address community questions about rising costs and project scope. In terms of public safety, Mayor Mees has taken a public-facing role during significant local incidents, such as providing official updates and confirmations during an investigation into a series of arsons that troubled the community.

# Involvement In Bartlett Isd Bond

Mayor Chad Mees was a key city official involved in the public discussions concerning the Bartlett ISD's proposed $16.8 million bond, which was intended to finish construction projects from a previous 2022 bond. His role was primarily to explain the city's influence on the school district's rising costs. At a community meeting, Mees addressed resident questions by clarifying the impact of the city's newly implemented building codes. He stated, “The city code team is new. Before that you could pull permit with the city for 10 bucks, no inspection, no rules to follow basically,” thereby linking the increased project costs to the more rigorous and necessary city regulations. This bond followed a 2022 bond for $20 million, which was insufficient to complete all projects due to these rising costs and code changes. Mees also attended a community town hall organized for residents to ask questions about the new bond proposal, positioning himself as a point of contact for the city's role in the matter.

# Public Safety Actions

In his capacity as mayor, Chad Mees has been directly involved in communicating with the public on significant safety issues. Following a series of fires in Bartlett, which included the destruction of a vacant building and damage to a church, Mayor Mees served as an official source of information. He publicly confirmed that investigators had identified a pattern connecting at least three of the four fires to a single suspect. His statement raised concerns about a potential "serial arsonist operating in the area" and highlighted the seriousness of the threat to the community. By providing these updates, he played a crucial role in keeping residents informed during an active and concerning criminal investigation.

# Controversies And Public Scrutiny

Chad Mees faced public scrutiny and resident frustration during a community town hall held on April 21 regarding the Bartlett ISD's $16.8 million bond proposal. The meeting was organized for residents to ask questions of both Mayor Mees and the school superintendent, Dr. Teddy Clevenger. However, the superintendent did not attend, and Mayor Mees arrived nearly 45 minutes late to the meeting. This left more than a dozen gathered residents with unanswered questions and led to frustration over communication gaps and a perceived lack of accountability from local leaders. The incident highlighted community skepticism about the new bond, which was proposed to cover costs for projects left incomplete from a 2022 bond. While Mees's role was as a city representative, his late arrival compounded the public's dissatisfaction with the handling of the bond issue.
