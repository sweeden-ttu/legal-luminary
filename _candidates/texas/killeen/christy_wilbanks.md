# Executive Summary

Christy Wilbanks is a candidate for one of three at-large seats on the Killeen City Council in the May 2, 2026, general election. Her campaign platform prioritizes attracting manufacturing and industry jobs to the city, working in collaboration with the Killeen Economic Development Corporation. She advocates for a cautious approach to new developments, such as a proposed data center, wanting to first assess impacts on infrastructure like water and electricity. Wilbanks also emphasizes the need to regularly update the city's comprehensive plan, ensure infrastructure is in place before approving growth, and increase government transparency through more frequent public updates on projects. A longtime resident with experience in county-level government, she previously ran for the District 4 council seat in May 2025.

# Political Platform And Priorities

Christy Wilbanks, a candidate for one of three Killeen City Council at-large seats in the May 2, 2026 election, has a platform centered on several key priorities. Her main goals include attracting larger manufacturing and industry jobs to Killeen to enable residents to live and work locally. She plans to collaborate with the Killeen Economic Development Corporation to achieve this. Another major priority is city planning and infrastructure; she advocates for regularly revisiting and updating the city's comprehensive plan, approximately every two years, to ensure it aligns with current needs. She strongly believes that essential infrastructure, particularly for drainage and flooding mitigation, must be established before approving new development projects. Additionally, Wilbanks is focused on increasing government transparency and communication, proposing more frequent public updates on city engineering and road projects.

# Economic Development Strategy

Christy Wilbanks' economic development strategy for Killeen focuses on attracting "bigger manufacturing and industry jobs" to ensure residents can "stay here, work here and live here." She intends to work closely with the Killeen Economic Development Corporation to achieve this goal. Wilbanks has specifically identified the automotive industry as a potential sector for the city to tap into. While she is pro-growth, she approaches new projects with caution. For instance, regarding a proposed data center, she expressed reservations, stating she is "a little bit hit or miss on that." Her concerns stem from the significant resources such projects consume, including water and electricity, as well as potential noise pollution, and she wants a thorough assessment of these impacts before offering her support.

# Views On Infrastructure And City Planning

Christy Wilbanks advocates for a proactive and structured approach to urban development and city planning in Killeen. A central tenet of her platform is the need to regularly revisit and update the city's comprehensive plan, suggesting a review cycle of "once every two years or so" to ensure it remains relevant. She is a firm proponent of ensuring infrastructure is in place before approving further growth. Wilbanks highlights existing problems, stating, "We’ve got areas now that are flooding," and insists, "We’ve got to redo that... have the infrastructure in place before building bigger." This 'infrastructure-first' policy extends to utilities and roads. Her caution regarding a proposed data center is also tied to this view, as she is concerned about its high consumption of water and electricity, which are key infrastructure components.

# Stance On Government Transparency

Christy Wilbanks has made increased government transparency a key part of her campaign platform. She believes the city can and should provide more information to the public. Specifically, she advocates for increasing the frequency of public updates on engineering and road projects from the current quarterly schedule to a monthly basis. She feels that while the city's website is a good tool, more information should be proactively published online. Her philosophy on the issue is summarized by her statement, "if you don’t have anything to hide, why not put it out?"
