# Executive Summary

Mellisa Brown is a former Killeen City Council member (served in 2022) and community advocate currently running for an at-large seat on the council in the May 2, 2026, general election. This follows an unsuccessful campaign for mayor in May 2024. Originally from Binghamton, New York, Brown moved to Killeen in 2008. Her campaign platform focuses on several key areas: increasing government transparency and citizen access by opposing the move of council meetings to less accessible afternoon times; implementing stricter fiscal management to address budget deficits and prioritize core services like public safety, drainage, and roads; and ensuring infrastructure development precedes new housing approvals, with a preference for planned unit developments to create larger, family-friendly lots. Brown is a prominent and often controversial figure in local politics, known for frequently clashing with the mayor and council members during public comment periods, which has led to her being arrested on two occasions after being removed from meetings.

# Views On City Governance

Mellisa Brown's views on city governance are centered on enhancing public accessibility and citizen participation. A primary focus of her platform is her strong opposition to the Killeen City Council's decision to move meetings from 5 p.m. to 3 p.m. on weekdays. She argues that this change effectively prevents most working residents from attending and participating in their local government. Brown has stated, “I’ve sat back over the last few years, and I have watched slowly the erosion of the rights of citizens and our ability to be heard.” Her advocacy includes pushing for a meeting structure and schedule that preserves and encourages public comment opportunities, ensuring that residents' voices are not marginalized. Her frequent and sometimes confrontational appearances at council meetings, including incidents leading to her removal and arrest, underscore her commitment to this cause.

# Views On Budget And Finance

Mellisa Brown expresses significant concern over Killeen's financial management, particularly regarding projected budget deficits. She advocates for a more fiscally responsible approach, stating, “we need council members who understand finance, who understand the budgeting process and can set priorities that will help us get out of that.” Her position is that by electing officials with strong financial acumen, the city can better navigate its budgetary challenges. Brown's stated priorities for city spending are clear and focused on core infrastructure and services. She believes the budget must be structured to ensure that essential areas like public safety, drainage, and roads are fully funded before other expenditures are considered.

# Views On Zoning And Development

In the realm of urban planning and development, Mellisa Brown advocates for a policy of 'infrastructure first.' She argues that essential infrastructure, such as roads and drainage, should be in place before the city approves new housing construction. This approach aims to manage growth responsibly and prevent strain on city services. Furthermore, Brown is a proponent of using more planned unit developments (PUDs). She believes this zoning tool can be leveraged to create more desirable, family-friendly neighborhoods by encouraging the development of larger residential lots.

# Interactions With City Council

Mellisa Brown has a history of contentious interactions with the Killeen mayor and city council. She is described as a frequent and vocal critic during the citizen comment portions of council meetings, leading to clashes with officials. These conflicts are rooted in policy and procedural disagreements. For example, a significant point of contention was the council's decision to move meetings from 5 p.m. to 3 p.m. and reduce their frequency, a move Brown publicly opposed, stating, “I’ve sat back over the last few years, and I have watched slowly the erosion of the rights of citizens and our ability to be heard.” Her confrontations have, on at least two occasions (in 2018 and 2024), escalated to the point where she was removed from the council chambers and subsequently arrested, highlighting the high-conflict nature of her engagement with the city's governing body.
