# Executive Summary

Jose Segarra, a former mayor of Killeen and long-time real estate broker, is a nonpartisan candidate for Mayor of Killeen in the general election scheduled for May 2, 2026. He is running against two other candidates, Riakos Adams and Joseph Solomon. Segarra's campaign is centered on his prior experience, arguing that he can be more effective in a second term without a 'learning curve.' His key priorities include strengthening trust and transparency in local government, attracting economic investment and opportunities by leveraging his existing relationships, and achieving measurable progress in public safety, infrastructure, and smart growth. He positions himself as a business owner with expertise in real estate and a deep understanding of the city's needs.

# Stance On Affordable Housing

Drawing on his expertise as a real estate agent, Jose Segarra has expressed skepticism about the suitability of tiny homes for the Killeen market. He argues that they are not a viable solution for the majority of local home buyers, who are predominantly families looking to purchase traditional houses. While he stated he would "favor condos more than tiny homes," he also noted that historically, condominiums have not sold well in the area because residents prefer to buy houses. His position emphasizes aligning housing solutions with the specific demands of the local market.

# Stance On Economic Development

Jose Segarra's approach to economic development centers on his experience as a business owner and his plan to leverage his extensive networks to attract investment and opportunities to Killeen. A key pillar of his platform is to bring more jobs, events, and entertainment to the city. He believes his prior experience as mayor allows him to be more effective from day one, without a "learning curve," in fostering economic growth.

# Stance On Fort Hood Relations

To improve support for soldiers and veterans and strengthen regional ties, Jose Segarra proposes reinstating regular meetings with the Fort Hood (Fort Cavazos) commander. His plan involves inviting the mayors of neighboring cities, such as Harker Heights and Copperas Cove, to join these meetings to better coordinate efforts and address the needs of the military community.

# Stance On Downtown Revitalization

Jose Segarra believes that successful downtown revitalization requires a focus on business viability before significant public investment. As a business owner, he argues that one "can’t just throw money at revitalization without improving the foot traffic." His stance is that efforts and funding should be directed at strategies to increase the number of people visiting downtown, which in turn will support and sustain local businesses, rather than relying solely on subsidies.

# Views On Government Transparency

Government transparency is a cornerstone of Jose Segarra's campaign. He has explicitly committed to ensuring the city complies with the Texas Public Information Act, stating that "compliance is not optional." In response to questions about city communication, he has promised that, as mayor, he would hold press conferences with appropriate city staff during major incidents to provide accurate and direct information to the public and media. He defines transparency as starting with "accessibility, accountability, and a willingness to communicate openly with the public."

# Controversies And Criticism

## Issue

Government Transparency and Public Information Requests

## Details

While not a direct controversy involving the candidate, Jose Segarra was questioned by the Killeen Daily Herald about a broader issue concerning the city administration's delays in fulfilling public information requests under the Texas Public Information Act. The issue was framed as a general challenge for the city that all candidates were asked to address.

## Candidate Response

Jose Segarra responded by outlining specific remedies he would implement as mayor. He affirmed that compliance with the Texas Public Information Act is 'not optional.' He committed to holding press conferences with appropriate city staff during major incidents to provide accurate information and answer questions directly. He summarized his stance by stating, 'Transparency starts with accessibility, accountability, and a willingness to communicate openly with the public.'
