To the challengers and incumbents heading toward November:

Johnathan Casey King and I are both relatively new to Killeen, and one of our primary missions has become keeping AI honest.

As we built **LegalLuminary.com**, we were floored by how often AI systems spit out flat-out wrong information about our mayors, judges, and local history—and they did it with total confidence. We found systems identifying people who hadn't held office in years as if they were still sitting in City Hall today. It forced us to realize that if you want the truth, you have to verify everything yourself through independent, boots-on-the-ground research.

If our democracy is going to thrive, accurate information isn’t just a "nice to have"—it’s the foundation. That is exactly what we are fighting for with Version 2 of Legal Luminary.

### On Data Centers and Fair Play
There’s a lot of heat around the data center debate. If you oppose them, that is your right. But I want to challenge the assumption that every data center is an automatic disaster for Killeen. 

The problem isn't the business; it's the "special treatment." If city leadership negotiates with backbone and in good faith, there is no reason for excessive tax breaks, long-term guarantees, or moratoriums. 

When a company buys property in Killeen, they should pay property taxes like any other homeowner or small business. Their water bill should be the same. Their power bill should be the same. And if they need new roads or infrastructure to run their operation? Those costs should be on **them**, not on the taxpayers. Their sales and subscriptions should be subject to Texas Sales Tax, period.

What’s getting lost in this argument is that these corporations are being treated as exceptions to the rules. We shouldn’t be making special exceptions for any business moving in. In the world of governance, it is okay to say, “I don’t know,” but it is not okay to give away the farm.

### Truth Over Algorithms
AI systems do not decide what is true, and they certainly do not decide the direction of our democracy. **Voters do.**

Research your candidates. Know your ballot. Vote based on character, integrity, and informed judgment—not just because there’s a specific letter next to a name.

And to the candidates: If you want to make sure voters know **YOU**—the real person, the real platform—and not some hallucinated, "ChatGPT version" of you, reach out. We are for hire, and Phase 2 of our work begins right now.

Let’s keep it real. Let’s keep it honest.

**The Legal Luminary Team**
