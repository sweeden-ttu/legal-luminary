**Headline:** Texas to set rules for using treated fracking water on farms

**Byline:** Alejandra Martinez

**Date:** 2026-06-12T10:00:00+00:00

**Article:**

The state’s environmental regulator will hold a public hearing June 15 on its proposed rules for how the toxic wastewater can be reused. Here’s what you should know.

The Texas Commission on Environmental Quality (TCEQ) is set to hold a public hearing on June 15 to discuss its proposed rules for using treated fracking wastewater on farmland. The hearing will provide an opportunity for the public to voice their opinions and concerns about the proposed regulations.

Fracking wastewater, also known as produced water, is the water that is brought up from deep underground during the hydraulic fracturing process. This water can contain various chemicals and contaminants, making it toxic and unsuitable for direct use on crops. However, with the right treatment, it can be made safe for agricultural use.

The proposed rules aim to ensure that any treated fracking wastewater used on farmland does not pose a risk to human health or the environment. They will cover aspects such as the types of treatments required, the monitoring and reporting of wastewater use, and the labeling of crops grown using treated water.

The public hearing will be an important opportunity for stakeholders, including farmers, environmental groups, and industry representatives, to engage with the TCEQ and provide input on the proposed regulations. The agency will use this feedback to refine its rules before finalizing them.

The Texas Tribune, a non-profit news organization, will cover the hearing and provide updates on the proposed rules and their potential impact on agriculture and the environment.
