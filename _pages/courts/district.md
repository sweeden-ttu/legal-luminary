# Bell County District Courts

<p class="intro-text">Six state district courts — the 27th, 146th, 169th, 264th, 426th and 478th — have jurisdiction in Bell County (<a href="https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/gv/htm/gv.24.htm#24.129">Gov't Code §24.129</a>) and sit at the Bell County Justice Center in Belton (<a href="https://www.bellcountytx.com/county_government/district_courts/426th_district_court/index.php">Bell County</a>). District judges are elected to four-year terms (<a href="https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/cn/htm/cn.5.htm#5.7">Tex. Const. art. V §7</a>); the 27th District is composed of Bell and Lampasas counties (<a href="https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/gv/htm/gv.24.htm#24.129">Gov't Code §24.129(a)</a>). Vacancies are filled by the Governor until the next general election (<a href="https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/cn/htm/cn.5.htm#5.28">Tex. Const. art. V §28</a>). Three seats — the 169th, 264th and 478th — are on the November 3, 2026 ballot, each with an unopposed Republican nominee (<a href="https://www.bellcountytx.com/departments/elections/ballot_samples.php">Bell County ballot samples</a>).</p>

## Associate judges and magistrates

Associate judges are appointed rather than elected: a judge of a court with family-law jurisdiction may appoint one if the commissioners court authorizes the position ([Family Code §201.001](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/fa/htm/fa.201.htm#201.001)), while associate judges for child-support (Title IV-D) and child-protection cases are appointed by the presiding judge of the administrative judicial region from applicants to the Office of Court Administration ([§201.101](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/fa/htm/fa.201.htm#201.101); [§201.201](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/fa/htm/fa.201.htm#201.201)).
