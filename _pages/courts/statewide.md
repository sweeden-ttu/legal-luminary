# Texas Supreme Court and Court of Criminal Appeals

<p class="intro-text">Texas has two courts of last resort, each with nine members elected statewide to six-year terms (<a href="https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/cn/htm/cn.5.htm#5.2">Tex. Const. art. V §§2, 4</a>): the <strong>Supreme Court</strong>, whose appellate jurisdiction covers all cases except criminal matters, and the <strong>Court of Criminal Appeals</strong> for criminal cases (<a href="https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/cn/htm/cn.5.htm#5.3">art. V §§3, 5</a>). Four Supreme Court seats (one an unexpired term) and three Court of Criminal Appeals seats were on the November 3, 2026 ballot, and every one was contested (<a href="https://www.bellcountytx.com/departments/elections/ballot_samples.php">Bell County ballot samples</a>). Democrats won all seven, unseating four Republican Supreme Court justices and Court of Criminal Appeals Judge Kevin Patrick Yeary, according to unofficial returns (<a href="https://electionresults.sos.state.tx.us/results.html">Texas Secretary of State</a>; <a href="https://www.texastribune.org/2026/11/04/texas-supreme-court-criminal-appeals-results-elections-2026/">Texas Tribune</a>). The Tribune reported that the results leave Republicans a 5–4 majority on the Supreme Court (<a href="https://www.texastribune.org/2026/11/04/texas-supreme-court-criminal-appeals-results-elections-2026/">Texas Tribune</a>).</p>
