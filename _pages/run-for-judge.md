<p class="intro-text">
You do not need anyone's appointment, endorsement or permission to run for judge in Texas. If you meet the constitutional qualifications for the office, you can put your name before the voters by filing with a party, as an independent, or as a write-in candidate. This page explains each route for Bell County's judicial offices and links every requirement to the law or official guidance that sets it.
</p>

<div class="legal-notice">
<strong>Not legal advice.</strong> This page is general information compiled from public sources and is not a substitute for the advice of an attorney. Filing rules, fees and deadlines change; confirm them with the Texas Secretary of State, your county party chair, or the Bell County Judge's office before you file.
</div>

## Do you need an appointment first?

**No.** Appointment is a separate way to fill a seat that becomes vacant between elections, and the appointee serves only until the next general election:

- **District and appellate judges** — the Governor fills the vacancy "until the next succeeding General Election for state officers, and at that election the voters shall fill the vacancy for the unexpired term" ([Tex. Const. art. V, §28(a)](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/cn/htm/cn.5.htm#5.28)).
- **Justices of the peace** — the Bell County Commissioners Court fills the vacancy "until the next succeeding General Election" ([Tex. Const. art. V, §28(b)](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/cn/htm/cn.5.htm#5.28); [Local Gov't Code §87.041](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/lg/htm/lg.87.htm#87.041)).
- **County courts at law** — the Commissioners Court fills a vacancy in a statutory county court judgeship, and the appointee serves until the next general election ([Gov't Code §25.0009](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/gv/htm/gv.25.htm#25.0009)).

An appointed judge therefore holds the seat only until the next general election, when anyone qualified can run for it.

## Who is eligible?

Every candidate for public office must meet the general requirements of [Texas Election Code §141.001](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/el/htm/el.141.htm#141.001): U.S. citizen; at least 18; not found by a final probate-court judgment to be totally mentally incapacitated (or partially incapacitated without the right to vote); not finally convicted of a felony unless pardoned or otherwise released from the resulting disabilities; 12 months' continuous residence in Texas and six months in the territory the office serves; and a registered voter. Judicial offices add their own requirements:

## Bell County's judicial offices

- **District Courts:** 27th (Bell and Lampasas counties — [Gov't Code §24.129](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/gv/htm/gv.24.htm#24.129)), and the 146th, 169th, 264th, 426th and 478th, each composed of Bell County alone ([Gov't Code §§24.247, 24.358, 24.441, 24.570, 24.60022](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/gv/htm/gv.24.htm#24.247))
- **County Courts at Law:** No. 1, No. 2 and No. 3 ([Gov't Code §25.0161](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/gv/htm/gv.25.htm#25.0161))
- **Justices of the Peace:** Precinct 1; Precinct 2; Precinct 3, Places 1 and 2; Precinct 4, Places 1 and 2 (Bell County court pages: [Pct. 1](https://www.bellcountytx.com/county_government/justice_of_the_peace/precinct_1/index.php), [Pct. 2](https://www.bellcountytx.com/county_government/justice_of_the_peace/precinct_2/index.php), [Pct. 3 Pl. 1](https://www.bellcountytx.com/county_government/justice_of_the_peace/precinct_3_place_1/index.php), [Pct. 3 Pl. 2](https://www.bellcountytx.com/county_government/justice_of_the_peace/precinct_3_place_2/index.php), [Pct. 4 Pl. 1](https://www.bellcountytx.com/county_government/justice_of_the_peace/precinct_4_place_1/index.php), [Pct. 4 Pl. 2](https://www.bellcountytx.com/county_government/justice_of_the_peace/precinct_4_place_2/index.php))

**Why this matters:** for the March 3, 2026 primaries, every Bell County district court and county court at law seat on the ballot drew a single Republican candidate and no Democrat ([KDH News, Dec. 2025](https://kdhnews.com/news/local/the-field-is-set-for-the-local-primaries/article_98ac69b7-e2c1-4622-b028-bf4d2e30c61b.html)).

## Route 1: Run in a party primary

**When to file.** The deadline is 6:00 p.m. on the second Monday in December of the odd-numbered year before the election, and applications may not be filed earlier than the 30th day before that deadline ([Election Code §172.023](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/el/htm/el.172.htm#172.023)). For the 2026 election the window was November 8 – December 8, 2025. **For the 2028 election, the same rule puts the window at November 13 – December 13, 2027.**

**Where to file** ([Election Code §172.022](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/el/htm/el.172.htm#172.022); [SOS primary guide](https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/candidates/guide/2026/demorrep2026.shtml)):

- **Justice of the Peace, County Court at Law, and the 146th, 169th, 264th, 426th and 478th District Courts** (districts made up only of Bell County) — the **Bell County chair** of your party.
- **27th District Court** (Bell and Lampasas) and the **Third Court of Appeals** — the **state chair** of your party.

**Filing fee** ([Election Code §172.024](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/el/htm/el.172.htm#172.024); [SOS primary guide](https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/candidates/guide/2026/demorrep2026.shtml)). The justice-of-the-peace fee is $1,000 in a county of 200,000 or more inhabitants under the 2020 Census and $375 in smaller counties; Bell County's 2020 Census population was 370,647 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_County,_Texas)), so the higher fee applies:

<div class="table-scroll" markdown="1">

| Office | Fee |
|---|---|
| Justice of the Peace | $1,000 |
| County Court at Law | $1,500 |
| District Judge | $1,500 |
| Third Court of Appeals | $2,500 (the higher court-of-appeals fee applies in Districts 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 14 and 15), plus a 250-signature petition |

</div>

**Or collect signatures instead of paying the fee** ([Election Code §172.025](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/el/htm/el.172.htm#172.025)). The petition needs the lesser of **500 signatures** or **2% of the votes cast for governor in 2022** in the territory the office serves ([SOS primary guide](https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/candidates/guide/2026/demorrep2026.shtml)). Bell County cast 87,936 votes for governor in 2022 ([Wikipedia, 2022 Texas gubernatorial election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Texas_gubernatorial_election)); 2% is about 1,759, so a **countywide or Bell-only district seat requires 500 signatures**. For a justice of the peace precinct, the number is 2% of that precinct's 2022 governor vote (never more than 500) — ask your county party chair for the exact figure. A Third Court of Appeals candidate using a petition in lieu of the fee needs 750 signatures ([SOS judicial guide](https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/candidates/guide/2026/judicial-office2026.shtml)).

**No extra "judicial petition" in Bell County.** The 250-signature judicial petition filed *in addition to* the fee is required only for the Courts of Appeals in the 1st, 2nd, 3rd, 4th, 5th, 14th and 15th districts and for judicial offices (including justice of the peace) in Bexar, Dallas, Harris and Tarrant counties ([SOS judicial guide](https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/candidates/guide/2026/judicial-office2026.shtml); [Election Code §172.021(e)](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/el/htm/el.172.htm#172.021), which keys the trial-court rule to counties of more than 1.5 million).

**Extra disclosures for lawyer-judges.** Candidates for district court, county court at law and the appellate courts must list their State Bar number, any public disciplinary sanctions, their legal practice and courtroom experience over the preceding five years, and any Class A or B misdemeanor conviction in the preceding ten years ([Election Code §141.0311](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/el/htm/el.141.htm#141.0311); [SOS judicial guide](https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/candidates/guide/2026/judicial-office2026.shtml)).

## Route 2: Run as an independent

([Election Code ch. 142](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/el/htm/el.142.htm); [SOS independent guide](https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/candidates/guide/2026/ind2026.shtml))

- **No filing fee option** — every independent candidate files a nominating petition with the application ([§142.004](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/el/htm/el.142.htm#142.004); [SOS independent guide](https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/candidates/guide/2026/ind2026.shtml)).
- **Declaration of intent** filed during the regular primary filing period ([§142.002](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/el/htm/el.142.htm#142.002)) — Nov. 8 to 6:00 p.m. Dec. 8, 2025 for the 2026 election. It is mandatory: the SOS says an application filed without it is rejected ([SOS independent guide](https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/candidates/guide/2026/ind2026.shtml)).
- **Application and petition** due by 5:00 p.m. on the 30th day after runoff primary election day ([§142.006](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/el/htm/el.142.htm#142.006)) — June 25, 2026 ([SOS independent guide](https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/candidates/guide/2026/ind2026.shtml)).
- **Signatures:** the lesser of 500 or 5% of the 2022 governor vote in the territory ([§142.007](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/el/htm/el.142.htm#142.007)) — **500** for a countywide seat in Bell County, since 5% of 87,936 exceeds 500.
- **Who can sign:** a signature is invalid if the signer voted in the general or runoff primary of a party that nominated a candidate for that office ([§142.009](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/el/htm/el.142.htm#142.009)). A person who voted in a primary, or ran in one, is ineligible to be an independent candidate for an office nominated in that primary ([§162.015](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/el/htm/el.162.htm#162.015)).
- **Where to file:** the **county judge** for county and precinct offices (justice of the peace, county court at law); the **Secretary of State** for district and state offices ([§142.005](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/el/htm/el.142.htm#142.005)).

## Route 3: Run as a write-in

([Election Code ch. 146](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/el/htm/el.146.htm); [SOS write-in guide](https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/candidates/guide/2026/writein2026.shtml))

- File a **declaration of write-in candidacy** with a filing fee equal to the primary fee for the same office ([§146.0231](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/el/htm/el.146.htm#146.0231)), or a petition in lieu of the fee with the same signature count as a primary petition ([§146.0232](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/el/htm/el.146.htm#146.0232)).
- **Deadline:** 5:00 p.m. on the 78th day before the general election, and no earlier than the 30th day before that deadline ([§146.025](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/el/htm/el.146.htm#146.025)) — July 18 to August 17, 2026 ([SOS write-in guide](https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/candidates/guide/2026/writein2026.shtml)).
- **Where to file:** the county judge for county and precinct offices; the Secretary of State for state and district offices ([§146.024](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/el/htm/el.146.htm#146.024)).
- **Not eligible:** someone who was a candidate in that office's primary ([§162.015(b)](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/el/htm/el.162.htm#162.015)), or who filed with more than one party in the same voting year ([§162.0151](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/el/htm/el.162.htm#162.0151); [SOS write-in guide](https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/candidates/guide/2026/writein2026.shtml)).
