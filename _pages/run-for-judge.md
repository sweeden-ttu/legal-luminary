<p class="intro-text">
You do not need anyone's appointment, endorsement or permission to run for judge in Texas. If you meet the constitutional qualifications for the office, you can put your name before the voters by filing with a party, as an independent, or as a write-in candidate. This page explains each route for Bell County's judicial offices and links every requirement to the law or official guidance that sets it.
</p>

<div class="legal-notice">
<strong>Not legal advice.</strong> This page is general information compiled from public sources and is not a substitute for the advice of an attorney. Filing rules, fees and deadlines change; confirm them with the Texas Secretary of State, your county party chair, or the Bell County Judge's office before you file.
</div>

## Do you need an appointment first?

**No.** Appointment is a separate way to fill a seat that becomes vacant between elections, and the appointee serves only until the next general election:

- **District and appellate judges** — the Governor fills the vacancy "until the next succeeding General Election for state officers, and at that election the voters shall fill the vacancy for the unexpired term" ([Tex. Const. art. V, §28(a)](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/cn/htm/cn.5.htm#5.28)).
- **Justices of the peace** — the Bell County Commissioners Court fills the vacancy "until the next succeeding General Election" ([Tex. Const. art. V, §28(b)](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/cn/htm/cn.5.htm#5.28); [Local Gov't Code §87.041](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._local_gov't_code_section_87.041)).

An appointed judge must then win an election to keep the seat — and anyone qualified can run against them.

## Who is eligible?

Every candidate for public office must meet the general requirements of [Texas Election Code §141.001](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/el/htm/el.141.htm#141.001): U.S. citizen; at least 18; not found by a final probate-court judgment to be totally mentally incapacitated (or partially incapacitated without the right to vote); not finally convicted of a felony unless pardoned or otherwise released from the resulting disabilities; 12 months' continuous residence in Texas and six months in the territory the office serves; and a registered voter. Judicial offices add their own requirements:

<div class="table-scroll" markdown="1">

| Office | Minimum age | Residency | Legal experience | Law |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Justice of the Peace** | 18 | Texas 12 months; precinct 6 months | **None — no law license required** | Tex. Const. art. V, §18 |
| **County Court at Law** | 25 | County 2 years | Licensed attorney, 4 years | Gov't Code §25.0014 |
| **District Judge** | 25 | District 2 years | 8 years as a practicing lawyer or judge; license not revoked or suspended | Tex. Const. art. V, §7; Gov't Code §24.001 |
| **Court of Appeals** | 35 | Texas 12 months; district 6 months | 10 years | Tex. Const. art. V, §6 |

</div>

Source: [Texas Secretary of State, Qualifications for All Public Offices (2026)](https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/candidates/guide/2026/qualifications2026.shtml).

## Bell County's judicial offices

- **District Courts:** 27th (Bell and Lampasas counties — [Gov't Code §24.129](https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/government-code/gov-t-sect-24-129/)), 146th, 169th, 264th, 426th and 478th
- **County Courts at Law:** No. 1, No. 2 and No. 3
- **Justices of the Peace:** Precinct 1; Precinct 2; Precinct 3, Places 1 and 2; Precinct 4, Places 1 and 2

See the [Judicial Officers directory](/candidates/courts/) for who holds each seat today.

**Why this matters:** for the March 3, 2026 primaries, every Bell County district court and county court at law seat on the ballot drew a single candidate ([KDH News, Dec. 2025](https://kdhnews.com/news/local/the-field-is-set-for-the-local-primaries/article_98ac69b7-e2c1-4622-b028-bf4d2e30c61b.html)). Voters had no choice in those races.

## Route 1: Run in a party primary

Most Texas judges reach the November ballot by winning the Republican or Democratic primary in March.

**When to file.** The deadline is 6:00 p.m. on the second Monday in December of the odd-numbered year before the election, and applications may not be filed earlier than the 30th day before that deadline ([Election Code §172.023](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/el/htm/el.172.htm#172.023)). For the 2026 election the window was November 8 – December 8, 2025. **For the 2028 election, the same rule puts the window at November 13 – December 13, 2027.**

**Where to file** ([Election Code §172.022](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/el/htm/el.172.htm#172.022); [SOS primary guide](https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/candidates/guide/2026/demorrep2026.shtml)):

- **Justice of the Peace, County Court at Law, and the 146th, 169th, 264th, 426th and 478th District Courts** (districts made up only of Bell County) — the **Bell County chair** of your party.
- **27th District Court** (Bell and Lampasas) and the **Third Court of Appeals** — the **state chair** of your party.

**Filing fee** ([Election Code §172.024](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._election_code_section_172.024)). Bell County's 2020 Census population was 370,647, so the higher justice-of-the-peace fee for counties of 200,000 or more applies:

<div class="table-scroll" markdown="1">

| Office | Fee |
|---|---|
| Justice of the Peace | $1,000 |
| County Court at Law | $1,500 |
| District Judge | $1,500 |
| Third Court of Appeals | $2,500, plus a 250-signature petition |

</div>

**Or collect signatures instead of paying the fee** ([Election Code §172.025](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/el/htm/el.172.htm#172.025)). The petition needs the lesser of **500 signatures** or **2% of the votes cast for governor in 2022** in the territory the office serves. Bell County cast 87,936 votes for governor in 2022; 2% is about 1,759, so a **countywide or Bell-only district seat requires 500 signatures**. For a justice of the peace precinct, the number is 2% of that precinct's 2022 governor vote (never more than 500) — ask your county party chair for the exact figure.

**No extra "judicial petition" in Bell County.** Texas requires judicial candidates to file a petition *in addition to* the fee only for appellate courts and for trial courts in Bexar, Dallas, Harris and Tarrant counties ([Election Code §172.021](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/el/htm/el.172.htm#172.021); [SOS judicial guide](https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/candidates/guide/2026/judicial-office2026.shtml)).

**Extra disclosures for lawyer-judges.** Candidates for district court, county court at law and the appellate courts must list their State Bar number, any public disciplinary sanctions, and their courtroom experience over the preceding five years ([Election Code §141.0311](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/el/htm/el.141.htm#141.0311)).

## Route 2: Run as an independent

([Election Code ch. 142](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/el/htm/el.142.htm); [SOS independent guide](https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/candidates/guide/2026/ind2026.shtml))

- **No filing fee** (§142.004).
- **Declaration of intent** filed during the same November–December window as the primaries. It is mandatory: an application without it is rejected.
- **Application and petition** due by 5:00 p.m. on the 30th day after the runoff primary (§142.006) — June 25 in 2026.
- **Signatures:** the lesser of 500 or 5% of the 2022 governor vote in the territory — **500** for a countywide seat in Bell County.
- **Who can sign:** registered voters in the territory who did **not** vote in the primary or runoff of a party that nominated a candidate for that office (§142.009). The candidate must not have voted in such a primary either.
- **Where to file:** the **Bell County Judge** for justice of the peace and county court at law; the **Secretary of State** for district judge.

## Route 3: Run as a write-in

([Election Code ch. 146](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/el/htm/el.146.htm); [SOS write-in guide](https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/candidates/guide/2026/writein2026.shtml))

- File a **declaration of write-in candidacy** with the same filing fee as a primary candidate, or a petition in lieu of the fee (lesser of 500 or 2% of the 2022 governor vote).
- **Deadline:** 5:00 p.m. on the 78th day before the general election (August 17 in 2026).
- **Where to file:** the Bell County Judge for justice of the peace and county court at law; the Secretary of State for district and appellate judges.
- **Not eligible:** someone who was a candidate in that office's primary (§162.015), or who filed with more than one party ([SB 901, §162.0151](https://www.sos.texas.gov/elections/candidates/guide/2026/writein2026.shtml)).

## Correcting the record

At least one widely used candidate site lists a **$375** filing fee and a **March–April 2026** filing period for Bell County justice of the peace. Neither is correct: the fee for a county of Bell's size is $1,000 under §172.024, and the primary filing period closed December 8, 2025 under §172.023. Always confirm requirements against the statute or the Secretary of State.
