# Nathan Johnson — Democratic nominee, elected Texas Attorney General

**Nathan Johnson** (Democrat) is a Texas state senator from Dallas (District 16) ([Texas Senate](https://senate.texas.gov/member.php?d=16)) and was the Democratic nominee for Attorney General in the **November 3, 2026** general election against Republican Mayes Middleton and Libertarian Tom Oxford ([Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Texas_Attorney_General_election,_2026)). He won the general election with 5,133,629 votes to Middleton's 4,502,442, according to unofficial statewide returns ([Texas Secretary of State](https://electionresults.sos.state.tx.us/results.html)). He won the May 26 runoff over former Galveston mayor Joe Jaworski, 60.5% to 39.5% ([Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Texas_Attorney_General_election,_2026); [KUT](https://www.kut.org/politics/2026-05-26/democrat-nathan-johnson-to-run-against-republican-mayes-middleton-for-texas-ag-in-november-election)). This page is informational only and does not endorse any candidate.

## Background

Johnson holds a physics degree from the University of Arizona and a law degree from the University of Texas at Austin, and is a litigator and mediator at Thompson Coburn LLP ([Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Nathan_Johnson_(Texas))).

## Legislative record

He was first elected to the Senate in 2018 ([Texas Senate](https://senate.texas.gov/member.php?d=16)) and assumed office on January 8, 2019, succeeding Republican Don Huffines; his committee assignments have included Business & Commerce, Jurisprudence, and Water, Agriculture & Rural Affairs ([Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Nathan_Johnson_(Texas))). His campaign website says he has passed 134 bills into law over four sessions ([Nathan Johnson campaign](https://nathanfortexas.com/priorities)); Legal Luminary has not independently verified that figure. See the [Texas Senate](https://senate.texas.gov/member.php?d=16) and the Legislature's records for bills and votes.

## Stated priorities

Per the [Texas Tribune](https://www.texastribune.org/2026/10/05/texas-attorney-general-election-2026-candidates-mayes-middleton-nathan-johnson-tom-oxford/), Johnson says he would reinvest in the office's core functions such as collecting child support and issuing legal opinions, enforce consumer-protection laws, cooperate with local district attorneys rather than seek more criminal-prosecution power, investigate and prosecute elected officials regardless of party, and sue the federal government over border policy and tactics he considers unconstitutional. An October 8 Texas Tribune profile described his pitch for a "back-to-basics" office and reported he said he would join lawsuits against the Trump administration only if necessary ([Texas Tribune via KSAT](https://www.ksat.com/news/texas/2026/10/08/democrat-nathan-johnson-pitches-back-to-basics-attorney-generals-office-after-12-years-under-paxton/)). Two former Texas Supreme Court justices appointed by Republican governors, Tom Phillips and Deborah Hankinson, endorsed him on Aug. 31, 2026 ([Texas Tribune](https://www.texastribune.org/2026/08/31/texas-attorney-general-gop-supreme-court-justices-endorse-democrat-nathan-johnson/)).

## Campaign finance (reported through June 30, 2026)

$290,566 cash on hand and $978,427 raised from January 1 to June 30, 2026; his largest listed donor was Texas Sands PAC ($120,000) ([Texas Tribune](https://www.texastribune.org/2026/10/05/texas-attorney-general-election-2026-candidates-mayes-middleton-nathan-johnson-tom-oxford/)).

## Why this race matters in Killeen and Bell County

The Attorney General's office defends state agencies and statutes in court, enforces child-support judgments, investigates deceptive businesses and charities, and issues opinions interpreting state law; Texas courts have said election-crime prosecutions belong to local prosecutors rather than the attorney general ([Texas Tribune](https://www.texastribune.org/2026/10/05/texas-attorney-general-election-2026-candidates-mayes-middleton-nathan-johnson-tom-oxford/)). Killeen has seen the office's reach firsthand: on January 31, 2024, Attorney General Ken Paxton sued Killeen and four other cities over their marijuana-decriminalization measures ([Killeen Daily Herald timeline](https://kdhnews.com/news/crime/a-timeline-of-turning-a-blind-eye-to-marijuana-possession-in-killeen/article_04628110-c223-11ee-a5bf-f7354cc25cd6.html)).
