# Mayes Middleton — Republican nominee for Texas Attorney General

**Mayes Middleton** (Republican) is a Texas state senator from Galveston (District 11) ([Texas Senate](https://senate.texas.gov/member.php?d=11)) and was the Republican nominee for Attorney General in the **November 3, 2026** general election against Democrat Nathan Johnson and Libertarian Tom Oxford ([Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Texas_Attorney_General_election,_2026)). Johnson won, 5,133,629 votes to Middleton's 4,502,442, according to unofficial statewide returns ([Texas Secretary of State](https://electionresults.sos.state.tx.us/results.html)). Middleton won the May 26 runoff over U.S. Rep. Chip Roy, 55.2% to 44.8% ([Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Texas_Attorney_General_election,_2026); [KUT](https://www.kut.org/politics/2026-05-26/democrat-nathan-johnson-to-run-against-republican-mayes-middleton-for-texas-ag-in-november-election)). This page is informational only and does not endorse any candidate.

## Background

Middleton is president of Middleton Oil Company and operates ranching, cattle and farming interests. He holds a bachelor's degree and a J.D. from the University of Texas at Austin ([Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Mayes_Middleton); [Texas Senate](https://senate.texas.gov/member.php?d=11)).

## Legislative record

He served in the Texas House (District 23) from 2019 to 2023 and has served in the Senate since January 10, 2023; he is not seeking re-election to his Senate seat in 2026 ([Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Mayes_Middleton)). He chaired the Texas House Freedom Caucus during his last House term ([Texas Senate](https://senate.texas.gov/member.php?d=11)). The Texas Tribune's February 2026 profile reported that his primary opponents said he lacked legal experience and that he had worked only in his family's oil and gas company; it said he passed a bill barring transgender athletes from high school and college sports as a senator, and that he has refiled every session a bill to bar local governments and school districts from using public funds to hire lobbyists ([Texas Tribune via KSAT](https://www.ksat.com/news/texas/2026/02/13/in-texas-attorney-general-race-mayes-middleton-runs-on-conservative-not-courtroom-record/)). For bills he authored and how he voted, see the [Legislature's authored-bills report](https://capitol.texas.gov/reports/report.aspx?LegSess=892&ID=author&Code=A1350) and the Senate journals; this page does not summarize bills whose numbers and outcomes have not been checked against those records.

## Stated priorities

Per the [Texas Tribune](https://www.texastribune.org/2026/10/05/texas-attorney-general-election-2026-candidates-mayes-middleton-nathan-johnson-tom-oxford/): enforcing the Trump administration's border-security and deportation agenda, upholding Texas abortion laws, protecting oil and gas jobs, preventing "foreign adversaries" from buying Texas property, increasing transparency in spending, and prosecuting violations of state law including election crimes. The Tribune notes that Texas courts have said election-crime prosecutions belong to local prosecutors.

## Campaign finance (reported through June 30, 2026)

$911,117 cash on hand; he has put more than $17 million of his own money into the race since launching in 2025. Other large donors include Court Reform Action for Families PAC ($150,000) and, at $100,000 each, Cody Campbell, Texans for Lawsuit Reform, Don Wasek and Kelcy Warren ([Texas Tribune](https://www.texastribune.org/2026/10/05/texas-attorney-general-election-2026-candidates-mayes-middleton-nathan-johnson-tom-oxford/)).

## Why this race matters in Killeen and Bell County

The Attorney General's office defends state agencies and statutes in court, enforces child-support judgments, investigates deceptive businesses and charities, and issues opinions interpreting state law; Texas courts have said election-crime prosecutions belong to local prosecutors rather than the attorney general ([Texas Tribune](https://www.texastribune.org/2026/10/05/texas-attorney-general-election-2026-candidates-mayes-middleton-nathan-johnson-tom-oxford/)). Killeen has seen the office's reach firsthand: on January 31, 2024, Attorney General Ken Paxton sued Killeen and four other cities over their marijuana-decriminalization measures ([Killeen Daily Herald timeline](https://kdhnews.com/news/crime/a-timeline-of-turning-a-blind-eye-to-marijuana-possession-in-killeen/article_04628110-c223-11ee-a5bf-f7354cc25cd6.html)).
