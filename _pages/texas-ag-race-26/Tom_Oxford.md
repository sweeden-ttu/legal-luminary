# Tom Oxford — Libertarian nominee for Texas Attorney General

**Tom Oxford** is the Libertarian nominee for Attorney General in the **November 3, 2026** general election, facing Democrat Nathan Johnson and Republican Mayes Middleton; he was nominated at the Libertarian Party convention on April 11, 2026 ([Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Tom_Oxford)). This page is informational only and does not endorse any candidate.

## Background

Oxford owns Oxford Law PLLC in Jefferson County and has practiced law since 1982 in personal injury, immigration and civil rights; he says he has argued before the Texas Supreme Court and the Fifth Circuit ([Libertarian Party](https://lp.org/candidate/tom-edward-oxford/); [Texas Tribune](https://www.texastribune.org/2026/10/05/texas-attorney-general-election-2026-candidates-mayes-middleton-nathan-johnson-tom-oxford/)). He holds a B.A. in political science from the University of Texas (1979) and a J.D. from the University of Houston Law Center (1982) ([Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Tom_Oxford)).

## Record and positions

Ballotpedia lists seven prior Libertarian runs for the Texas Supreme Court between 2008 and 2022, none successful ([Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Tom_Oxford)). The Texas Tribune found no public information on his specific policy positions and pointed instead to the Libertarian Party of Texas platform, which emphasizes limited government and individual rights; through June 30, 2026 he had reported $394 raised and $0 cash on hand ([Texas Tribune](https://www.texastribune.org/2026/10/05/texas-attorney-general-election-2026-candidates-mayes-middleton-nathan-johnson-tom-oxford/)).

## Why this race matters in Killeen and Bell County

The Attorney General's office defends state agencies and statutes in court, enforces child-support judgments, investigates deceptive businesses and charities, and issues opinions interpreting state law; Texas courts have said election-crime prosecutions belong to local prosecutors rather than the attorney general ([Texas Tribune](https://www.texastribune.org/2026/10/05/texas-attorney-general-election-2026-candidates-mayes-middleton-nathan-johnson-tom-oxford/)). Killeen has seen the office's reach firsthand: on January 31, 2024, Attorney General Ken Paxton sued Killeen and four other cities over their marijuana-decriminalization measures ([Killeen Daily Herald timeline](https://kdhnews.com/news/crime/a-timeline-of-turning-a-blind-eye-to-marijuana-possession-in-killeen/article_04628110-c223-11ee-a5bf-f7354cc25cd6.html)).
